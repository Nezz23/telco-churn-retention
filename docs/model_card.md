## Intended use
Rank existing telecom customers for a paid retention offer, and decide per customer whether the offer is worth its cost. The output is a calibrated churn probability, an expected-value decision and the top reasons.

**Not for:** pricing, credit or eligibility decisions; customers outside a consumer telecom base like the training data; estimating whether an offer *changes* behaviour (that needs uplift modelling on A/B campaign data).

## Calibration and decision threshold (calibration split only)
Calibrator chosen by 5-fold CV log loss: **sigmoid** (identity 0.4042, sigmoid 0.4038, isotonic 0.4143). Test ECE: 0.022 raw → 0.019 calibrated.

Decision rule: contact when expected value = P(churn) × save rate × 12 × monthly bill − offer cost exceeds a margin. The margin was tuned on cross-fitted calibrated probabilities: **$0** ($0 is the Bayes-optimal margin for perfectly calibrated probabilities; a tuned margin replaces it only if its gain has a paired-bootstrap 95% lower bound above zero. Best grid margin $10: gain $259, lower bound −$1,428). Test profit at the tuned margin: $29,660; at $0: $29,660.

ECE intervals sit above the point estimate: resampling duplicates customers, which inflates binned calibration error, so read them as conservative.

## Explanations
Per-customer reasons are exact SHAP values in log-odds (TreeSHAP for XGBoost), with one-hot columns summed back to their source feature. Features whose association exceeds 0.5 (|Spearman|, Cramér's V or correlation ratio, average linkage) form a group. Read credit at the group level: SHAP splits credit among correlated features arbitrarily.

## Limitations
- One static snapshot: no temporal validation is possible, and the drift section is a simulation.
- Campaign economics are assumptions (see the sensitivity grid in the app).
- The model predicts churn, not the causal effect of an offer.
- SHAP explains the model, not the customer: it is not causal.
